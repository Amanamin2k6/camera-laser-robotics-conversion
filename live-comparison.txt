LIVE WOLFRAM WEBSITE VS. AI WEB CONVERSION
Tested September 30, 2026 by OpenAI Codex.

Original live demonstration:
https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/MeasuringDistanceAndOrientationUsingCameraAndLasers/
Public conversion:
https://amanamin2k6.github.io/camera-laser-robotics-conversion/camera-laser-app/dist/

METHOD
Both live sites were actually operated in the in-app browser. Wolfram sliders,
the orientation dial and the object locator were used, and corresponding
controls were changed in the conversion. Comparison used settled graphics,
screenshots and publicly rendered SVG coordinates, rather than only source.
Desktop Mathematica was not installed or executed.

Original cloud graphics can lag the displayed slider value. An immediate new
control readout was not treated as proof that the graphic had finished updating.
The original dial landed at 50.625 degrees; that actual snapped angle was
entered in the conversion. The near-camera center was reconstructed from four
rendered original vertices. Those SVG values are rounded, limiting precision.

SIX PAIRED TESTS

1. Default: center (z,x)=(3,-0.2), theta=0, s=1, lambda=0.5.
   Original rays hit depth 2.5; conversion displayed 2.500 for both.
   Normalized horizontal spots were u=(-0.2,+0.2) in both.
   Original projected front face: 225 by 112.5 SVG units, aspect ratio 2:1.
   Conversion front face: 400 by 140, ratio approximately 2.857:1.

2. Maximum focal length: same scene, lambda=2.
   Both rendered spots at u=(-0.8,+0.8), the image boundaries.
   Both enlarged the cuboid and narrowed the top-view field-of-view cone.
   Conversion correctly kept physical hit depths at 2.500.

3. Minimum calibration: default center/angle, s=lambda=0.1.
   Both rendered spots at u=(-0.004,+0.004).
   Original adapted its major reticule labels to approximately 0.016,0.02,
   0.024,0.03,0.04,0.06,0.1,0.2, with negative counterparts and some fractions.
   Conversion retained 0.8,1,1.2,1.5,2,3,5,10,20,50 on both sides.
   Its labels clustered at the vanishing point. Original labels also overlap;
   adaptive scaling does not make the original reticule flawless.

4. Rotation: center (3,-0.2), theta=50.625 degrees, s=lambda=0.1.
   Original SVG-derived u=(-0.00541511,+0.00478578).
   Conversion u=(-0.00541625,+0.00478470).
   Maximum difference: approximately 0.00000114 image units, consistent with
   rounded original SVG coordinates. Original top-view ray endpoints indicated
   depths about 1.846 and 2.090, matching the displayed conversion depths.
   Conversion displayed angle 50.63 degrees; original has no such result card.

5. Two misses: same center/rotation, s=2, lambda=0.1.
   Both top views showed rays passing the cuboid. Original retained two tiny
   dots near the vanishing point, at SVG-derived u approximately +/-0.0000213.
   Its notebook uses placeholder endpoint depth 10000 for missing hits.
   Conversion showed no dots, two No hit readings and Unavailable orientation.

6. Near-camera drag: reconstructed center approximately (0.229843,0.884660),
   theta=50.625, s=2, lambda=0.1. The object crosses the camera plane.
   Both lasers missed. Original drew a brown face across much of the image;
   one vertex reached x=-71144.9 SVG units. Conversion omitted brown projected
   faces. Original clamps projection depth to 0.001; conversion skips faces
   with corners at or behind its near plane. Both scenes are physically invalid.

ENUMERATED DIFFERENCES

1. Incorrect camera-image aspect ratio in conversion. Original uses equal
   image-axis scaling. Conversion vertical scale is only 70% of horizontal
   scale, making the cuboid too squat. Test 1 directly confirms this.

2. Missing perspective floor grid. Original has projected floor lines and
   vanishing-line depth cues. Conversion uses a flat tinted lower image region.

3. Different reticule scaling. Original adapts tick depths when s*lambda
   changes; conversion keeps a fixed list. Both can have crowded labels.

4. Different reticule signs. Original negative-u labels are negative;
   positive-u labels are positive. Conversion uses positive forward depths on
   both sides and explains that convention. This is a convention change, not
   proof that its physical distances or mirrored projection are wrong.

5. Different orientation control and resolution. Original is a radian/pi dial
   snapping by pi/32 (5.625 degrees); conversion uses a degree slider stepping
   by 1 degree. The source defines that snap and the live dial produced 50.625.
   The two interfaces cannot select exactly the same angle set via their sliders.

6. Number/slider disagreement in conversion. When matching 50.625 degrees,
   the number field and geometry used 50.625, but the angle slider DOM value
   was 51. This extends the previously documented input-consistency problem.

7. Missing geometric annotations. Original shows an object angle arc and
   s/lambda dimension arrows. Conversion substitutes explanatory text.

8. Added position controls and presets. Original uses an object locator;
   conversion also has position number fields/sliders, Reset scene and three
   presets. These additions help reproduce tests, though typing bugs remain.

9. Different spot sizes. At default, original radius is 9 on a 450-wide plot;
   conversion radius is 10 on an 800-wide plot. Relative to image width,
   conversion radius is 62.5% of original radius, not an equivalent resize.
   Both renderers have visibility limits for very small dots: original miss
   dots had radius 1, and conversion normal dots have a minimum radius of 3
   in their respective SVG coordinate systems. Raw pixel sizes must therefore
   be normalized before comparing them.

10. Better missing-hit indication in conversion. Original keeps distant
    placeholder dots and has no No hit cards; conversion removes them and
    explicitly identifies unavailable measurements. This difference is useful.

11. Different near-plane failures. Original can stretch a face enormously;
    conversion can omit it entirely. Neither fully handles the physically
    invalid camera-plane-crossing scene. Original should not be assumed correct.

12. Added numeric result table and angle estimate. Original provides the
    reticule and explanatory formulas, not equivalent result cards. The
    previously documented 60-to-50.94-degree estimator error is in a feature
    added by the conversion; original does not display a numeric angle result
    with which that value could be directly compared.

13. Original website actions are omitted. Snapshot tabs, cloud/notebook/source
    actions and fullscreen demonstration mode are not reproduced. Conversion
    offers separate source-file/report links. These are interface differences,
    not errors in ray-intersection arithmetic.

14. Update timing differs. Original cloud graphics sometimes lag slider
    readouts; conversion updates SVG immediately in the browser. Comparing a
    transient original graphic with its new readout could create false errors.

ENUMERATED MATCHES

1. Default object placement, footprint, rotation, laser offset and focal length.
2. Default hit depth 2.5, correctly distinguished from center depth 3.
3. Mirrored horizontal projection in the four tested observable-hit scenes.
4. Focal-length scaling of spots/object and field-of-view narrowing/widening.
5. Rotated footprint and nearest ray intersections in the tested rotated pose.
6. Top-view rays pass the object in the tested two-miss case in both sites.

EVIDENCE AND LIMITS
evidence/live-comparison/live-observations.json contains paired SVG coordinates,
control values and conversion result text for the six cases. Paired screenshots
cover minimum, rotation, misses and near-camera cases. The default is recorded
in the paired SVG log. original-intermediate-render.jpg illustrates an original
graphic captured during queued updates; it is not a settled default comparison.

Matching SVG-derived horizontal coordinates supports these specific scenes,
not every continuous pose. The prior 990-ray independent analytical check is
a separate test; it is not 990 comparisons against the live Wolfram website.
