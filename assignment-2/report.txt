WHAT WAS IMPROVED AND HOW IT WAS VALIDATED

This is an engineering revision of the original conversion, not a repeat of
its fault report. The geometry was checked against the actual Mathematica
notebook. Input handling, measurement validity, rendering and interaction
were revised, then tested by operating the final browser application.

The retained model uses a 1-deep, 4-wide, 2-high cuboid; its rotated center is
(z,x). The camera is at z=0 and lasers start at lateral positions +s and -s,
so their separation is 2s. Projection is u=-lambda*x/z, v=lambda*y/z. The
sensor image bounds are u=+/-0.8 and v=+/-0.4. Distances are forward axial
depths to surface intersections, not object-center or camera slant distances.

Testing performed on the final implementation:

1. 20,560 laser rays were compared with an independent ray/box slab algorithm
   in cuboid-local coordinates. Hit/miss results and depths agreed within
   1e-8 units. The grid includes original pi/32 angles, additional +/-30 and
   +/-60 degree cases, near-camera scenes, calibration extremes and 2,000
   deterministic randomized scenes. These are calculation checks, not
   20,560 live-Wolfram comparisons.

2. 748 usable common-face angle results were checked against analytical face
   inclinations. 12,100 projected face polygons were checked for finite
   coordinates and correct image clipping. Additional closed-form and
   regression checks cover defaults, +/-30 degrees, focal scaling, two
   misses, different faces, half-turn symmetry, image boundaries, invalid
   camera-plane configurations, spot overlap and adaptive calibration.

3. 32 browser cases passed in Chrome and the same 32 passed in Edge. Controls
   were actually operated: character-by-character typing, Enter/blur/Escape,
   keyboard sliders, mouse dragging, dial rotation, presets, JSON download,
   scene-link copying and clipboard-denial fallback. Mobile tests used
   390x844 touch emulation, plus 320- and 768-pixel viewport checks. Touch
   dragging and keyboard movement worked; there was no page-level horizontal
   overflow. No application exceptions or failed application assets occurred
   during these cases. The detailed table scrolls within its own container.

4. Four fresh paired comparisons were performed against the actual live
   Wolfram demonstration: defaults, maximum focal length, minimum calibration,
   and a 50.625-degree original-dial rotation. The original sliders and dial
   were operated and settled SVG output was inspected. All eight normalized
   spot positions agreed within 0.00000115 image units. Relative spot radii
   agreed within 0.000000005 of image width. Rotated face vertices agreed
   within 0.00000103 image units. Original SVG rounding limits these figures.
   The default projected face is 2:1 in both versions, fixing the Assignment 1
   distortion. Ideal mode was selected for comparisons whose spots overlap.

5. Eight spot coordinates from the earlier stored live-Wolfram observations
   were also checked independently. These historical observations are kept
   separate from the four fresh comparisons. Desktop Mathematica and real
   camera/laser hardware were not executed or tested.

2. ENUMERATED LIST OF ALL IDENTIFIED REMAINING FAULTS / LIMITATIONS

1. Full object orientation remains unobservable from these two measurements.
   In the tested 180-degree scene, the two depths are 4.500 and the measured
   face inclination is 0 degrees, just as at 0 degrees. This is a symmetry
   limitation of the measurement setup, not an incorrect angle calculation.
   The interface now explicitly labels the result as face tilt modulo 180
   degrees and does not claim to recover the full signed object pose.

2. The sampling check is approximate rather than a physical camera model.
   It checks original-style circular spot overlap and one-pixel center
   separation at the selected resolution. It does not simulate image noise,
   lens distortion, finite laser divergence, intensity, calibration error,
   occlusion by other objects, centroid extraction or measurement uncertainty.
   For example, the far-distance scene is conservatively rejected as
   unresolved; this does not establish an actual camera's detection limit.
   The app describes this limitation and offers an explicitly labeled ideal
   geometry mode for comparison with the original.

3. Field-of-view validity uses the spot center. At the tested maximum focal
   length, the default spot centers lie exactly at u=+/-0.8 and remain usable
   under the ideal point-projection rule, although each drawn circular marker
   is partly clipped by the image boundary. Real recovery from a partially
   imaged spot may be biased or impossible. The final app has no separate
   uncertainty model for that partial-spot case.

4. Extremely small spots and dense reticule detail cannot all be inspected
   simultaneously at normal size. At small s and lambda, or large depth,
   spots are small by the original model and some tick labels would collide.
   The final renderer culls overlapping labels and offers 2x/4x/8x image crops,
   numeric readings and full-precision export. This improves readability but
   deliberately shows fewer labels than the original in crowded scenes.

5. The conversion is not a pixel-for-pixel copy of the Wolfram presentation.
   Colors, typography and control layout differ. The top view fits the whole
   object instead of retaining the original's fixed clipping window; crowded
   labels are culled. Wolfram snapshot tabs, notebook-cloud integration and
   Mathematica-specific authoring actions are not reproduced. The interactive
   geometry and standalone source are provided instead.

6. Calculations use JavaScript double-precision arithmetic rather than exact
   symbolic arithmetic. Displayed measurements are also rounded. Normal
   typed values are retained at JavaScript numeric precision; dragging is
   rounded to six decimal places for usable controls. Full stored readings
   are available on hover and through JSON export. Very close edge contacts
   are resolved using numerical tolerances, so this is not an exact symbolic
   treatment of every singular configuration.

7. Validation is finite. Chrome and Edge were tested, including emulated
   mobile touch, but Firefox, Safari, physical phones and assistive devices
   were not independently tested. Keyboard operation and accessible labels
   were checked, but a full screen-reader usability audit was not performed.
   Real optics and desktop Mathematica were not tested. The reported test
   results should not be read as universal equivalence or hardware accuracy.

3. ENUMERATED LIST OF WHAT WORKS WELL IN THE FINAL VERSION

1. Standalone web operation. The application loads and updates entirely in
   the browser, without Mathematica or a remote calculation service.

2. Correct defaults and dimensions. The default center (3,-0.2), orientation
   0 degrees, offset s=1, focal length 0.5, and 1x4x2 cuboid match the source.

3. Correct rotated geometry and nearest forward intersections. The independent
   20,560-ray comparison validates hit/miss and depth over the tested scenes.

4. Correct pinhole projection and calibration. Default image coordinates are
   -0.2 and +0.2, with recovered surface depths 2.500. Changing focal length
   scales image positions without changing the physical intersection depth.

5. Correct image-axis proportions. Horizontal and vertical image coordinates
   now use the same scale. The default front face has the correct 2:1 ratio,
   including at mobile widths; the Assignment 1 ratio of 2.857 was fixed.

6. Correct positive and negative same-face tilt. At center (5,0), theta=+30,
   s=1 and lambda=0.5, depths are 3.845299 and 5.000000 and tilt is +30 degrees.
   At -30 degrees, the depths swap and tilt is -30 degrees.

7. Different-face rejection. At the former failing theta=60 scene, depths
   remain correctly 3.267949 and 5.732051, but tilt is now Unavailable with
   an explanation. The erroneous 50.94-degree chord estimate is not shown.
   The student-observed (z,x)=(4,0), theta=50.625 case is also rejected instead
   of presenting its 47.69-degree chord slope as valid face tilt.

8. Visibility-aware camera readings. The out-of-frame scene retains its
   geometric intersections in the table but withholds off-image camera depths
   and tilt. The earlier false result based on u=+/-4 is fixed. A separate
   student-style single-miss/off-image configuration also reports each status
   correctly rather than treating its one off-image depth as usable.

9. Clear handling of actual misses. One miss shows one valid depth and one
   No hit; two misses show two No hit entries. Neither gives a tilt estimate.
   Missed rays do not create the original's artificial z=10000 image dots.

10. Explicit invalid-scene handling. A cuboid crossing or surrounding the
    camera plane triggers a warning and no recovered camera measurements.
    Visible face portions are clipped before projection, and projected
    polygons remain finite and within the image. The former silent blank-face
    or enormous unbounded near-plane projection is not treated as valid.

11. Negative and decimal typing. Ordinary character-by-character entry of
    -30, -1 and 0.75 preserves the requested sign and decimal value. Fields
    retain draft text until Enter or blur, fixing the Assignment 1 input bug.

12. Clear, safe input validation. Deleting a depth value does not move the
    object. Entering 99 leaves the existing scene unchanged and explains the
    valid range. Escape restores the previous value. Incomplete or invalid
    drafts are not silently interpreted as zero or clamped into another scene.

13. Consistent controls and exact entry. Numeric fields, sliders and geometry
    agree at 50.625 degrees. Exact entries beyond six decimal places are also
    retained. Calibration keys change s and lambda by 0.01; angle keys and
    dial dragging use the original 5.625-degree increment.

14. Usable object movement. Mouse dragging, emulated touch dragging and arrow
    keys move the cuboid and update geometry, projection and readings. The
    handle has an enlarged interaction target, visible focus and an accessible
    label. Dragged positions avoid long uncontrolled floating-point strings.

15. Original explanatory guides restored. The top view includes the angle
    arc, orientation references, s and lambda dimension arrows, camera body,
    image planes and field-of-view cone. The camera view includes the source's
    projected floor grid, horizon cues and calibrated signed reticule.

16. Faithful reticule and spot scaling. Reticule depth scales follow the
    original piecewise lambda*s rule, with negative labels at negative u.
    Adaptive label culling prevents the tested overlaps. Relative spot radii
    match the fresh live-original comparison instead of the old incorrect
    scaling or forced minimum-radius enlargement.

17. Useful handling of unresolved spots. The default sampling check withholds
    separate measurements and tilt when spots merge or become subpixel. Image
    zoom does not falsely improve sensor validity. Ideal mode is explicitly
    labeled and reproduces the original exact-coordinate calculation.

18. Responsive feedback and layout. The live measurement strip stays visible
    while scrolling. Tested 320-, 390- and 768-pixel layouts do not overflow
    the page horizontally. The readings table scrolls within its own region.
    Plot labels keep a useful screen-space size rather than shrinking with
    the original fixed 800-unit drawing.

19. Reproducible scene presets and links. Eight presets exercise useful and
    failing configurations. Scene links preserve geometry, calibration,
    sampling mode and view crop. Invalid URL parameters are identified and
    replaced with defaults for those parameters. Reset restores every setting.

20. Full-precision measurement export. JSON includes the exact stored scene,
    intersections, image coordinates, visibility statuses and inference result.
    Recomputing the far-scene inverse from the exported coordinate gives 14.5
    rather than the old rounded-coordinate discrepancy of 14.49275.

21. Clear educational explanation. The app identifies axial depth, total laser
    separation 2s, mirrored projection, the same-face condition, orientation
    ambiguity and the distinction between simulated geometry and usable
    camera measurements. Warnings state why an estimate is unavailable.

22. Complete source and evidence. The download contains the application,
    geometry module, styles, icon, local server, verification scripts, original
    Mathematica source, reference observations and final report. Attribution
    and license links are included. The Assignment 1 application and its
    reports remain preserved at their original paths for comparison.
