Inspect the measurements

LaserGeometric zImage uCamera zStatus

Geometric z is the simulated axial depth to a surface, not the object-center depth or the slant distance from the camera. Camera z is recovered only from usable image spots. Hover over a number or export the readings for full precision.

Face tilt is an undirected inclination, modulo 180°. It cannot distinguish the cuboid’s symmetric orientations and is shown only when two usable hits share one face.

How the measurement works
PROJECT A HIT

u = −λx / z

The pinhole mirrors the lateral position.
RECOVER ITS DEPTH

z = −λx / u

The laser is at x = +s or x = −s.
INFER FACE TILT

atan2(zright − zleft, 2s)

Two visible, separate hits on the same planar face.

The cuboid is 1 unit deep, 4 wide and 2 high. Its center is (z, x); the camera is at z = 0, midway between the lasers. The total laser separation is 2s. All lengths use consistent arbitrary units. The image follows the original convention u = −λx/z, v = λy/z, with bounds ±0.8 horizontally and ±0.4 vertically.

Reticule signs match the original image-side convention: negative labels at negative u and positive labels at positive u. Both lasers’ physical forward depths are positive. The perspective floor is at y = −1. “Ideal geometry” uses exact ray coordinates, as the original does; it does not simulate camera noise or infer a pose from real photographs.
